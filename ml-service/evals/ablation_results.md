## Reading the table

Every figure is read from the recordings in the table above. Since phase 3c `evidence_cited` is one synthesis-level rule for both arms (the synthesis text must show the golden file's two synthesis keys, `fee.total_fee_paid_usd` and `risk.top_hour_trade_fraction`, whose rendered values are unique within each packet). Re-scored on the same recordings, the arms now tie on `invariants (6)` at 45 of 54 (83.3%) each: both fail `evidence_cited` in 9 of 9 runs and pass the other five. Before 3c the Council read 100.0% only because its `evidence_cited` was per agent, a different and easier test. The Council-only `agent evidence` column (each specialist shows its own key) is 0 of 9, so on the stronger keys neither arm's text cites the total fee or the top-hour share; the keys were chosen by uniqueness before looking at any recording, not tuned. On first-pass grounding violations the like-for-like comparison is the synthesis call alone (the all-agents column pools seven Council texts against one single-call text): 4.11 per run for the Council against 5.67 for the single call (lower is better). By user the Council is better on demo-moderate (2.00 against 9.00) and demo-disciplined (5.67 against 7.33), and the single call is better on demo-aggressive (0.67 against 4.67). Redactions were 1.00 per run for the Council (all in demo-disciplined) and 0.00 for the single call. The single call was as good as or better than the Council on rating agreement across runs (9 of 9 pairs agree against 5 of 9 for the Council) and on findings: both arms proposed 16 findings and the verifier passed none of them in either arm, so neither arm produced a material, replay-verified finding. Rating agreement says only that the single call repeats itself; it does not say the repeated rating is right. The Council ran 13.3 LLM calls per run against 2.6 for the single call (5.2x), with 3.7x the input tokens, 5.2x the output tokens and 6.5x the wall time. The Council's calls include 429 retries and pacing waits on the free tier, so its wall-time and call ratios are inflated by rate limiting.

## Limitations

- n = 3 runs per user per arm, 3 synthetic demo users (trader behaviour is synthetic; market data is real). Nine runs per arm is too few to call any difference between the arms significant, and the rating-agreement figures rest on 3 pairs per user.
- Free-tier models only (Groq, per `config/llm_routes.yaml`), with rate-limit retries counted as calls. Results may not carry over to a paid model.
- Both arms share the whole verification pipeline (grounding, the replay gate, the claim check, the cost override). It forces every number to come from code, which narrows the difference between the arms by design. Quality differences in the specialists' and debate's own prose are largely outside what the invariants measure.
- The invariants test grounding and wording, not whether a rating is correct: there are no ground-truth ratings, only allowed bands per user.
- `evidence_cited` is now the same synthesis-level rule for both arms; the required keys are chosen by hand for uniqueness. `demo-disciplined` has no unique liquidity value (all slippage is zero), so its liquidity specialist has no required key. The single-call prompt now has its own `SINGLE_PROMPT_VERSION` (1.0.0), which only recordings made after 3c carry; the existing recordings were not edited.
- The single-call prompt shares all writing and safety rules with the Council judge prompt (`agents/prompts.py`) but has its own framing, so the arms differ in more than the number of calls: the Council judge also sees specialist signals and the debate.
- The older Council runs (disciplined 1-2, moderate 1) predate the recording metadata block; the rest carry the arm, prompt version and routes. `PROMPT_VERSION` stayed 1.5.0 because the Council prompt text is byte-identical after the refactor (a test locks its hash).
