## 2. Crypto-specific execution research

- **Square-root impact on Bitcoin.** Donier and Bonart, "A million metaorder analysis of market impact on the Bitcoin", arXiv:1412.4503 [15]. Main findings from the abstract: square-root impact holds for Bitcoin/USD; it holds during the whole metaorder trajectory; the uninformed component accounts for almost all of the long-term decay of impact; the authors favour the latent order book model [15]. I did not read the full text for the numerical constants.
- **Trade direction.** Binance reports whether the taker is buyer or seller on each trade, so Lee-Ready inference is not needed there. An arXiv paper on perpetual futures uses this to estimate effective spreads and reports median effective spreads of 0.11 bps (BTC), 0.18 (ETH), 0.44 (BNB), 1.35 (DOGE), 2.12 (ADA) for the perpetual [16]. These are perpetual-futures numbers, not spot.
- **Accuracy of inferred direction.** I found no published Binance-specific Lee-Ready accuracy study. A 2026 arXiv paper on Polymarket cites roughly 80% Lee-Ready accuracy on Nasdaq [secondary, via search; not opened]. UNVERIFIED for crypto. Practical point: on Binance, Bybit and Coinbase the exchange labels the aggressor, so inference is only needed on venues without a label [31][33][38].
- **Maker fill probability, adverse selection, post-fill return.** arXiv:2502.18625 (Oxford and Queen Mary) ran a live maker-order experiment on the Binance linear BTC perpetual. It reports a trade-off between fill probability and post-fill performance, with the exchange taker fee as profitability threshold [17]. I did not extract its numbers.
- **Cross-asset limit order book study.** arXiv:2602.00776 uses Binance Futures order book and trade data at 1-second frequency, January 2022 to October 2025. It touches adverse selection but is not a markout study [18].
- **Retail execution costs.** See section 4.
- Not found: a peer-reviewed spot-market study of effective spread on Binance or Bybit using the public aggTrades archive. I did not search exhaustively.

## 3. Regulation and standards practice

### MiCA Article 78 (firms, not users)
- Article 78 text (ESMA Interactive Single Rulebook) [19]:
  - Providers must take all necessary steps to get the best possible result for clients. Factors named: price, costs, speed, likelihood of execution and settlement, size, nature, custody conditions, other relevant factors. Does not apply where the client gives specific instructions [19].
  - They must set up effective execution arrangements and an order execution policy [19].
  - They must explain the policy to clients and obtain prior consent [19].
  - They must be able to show clients on request, and the competent authority on request, that orders followed the policy [19].
  - They must monitor the policy, fix deficiencies, and regularly assess whether listed venues give the best result [19].
- The text I read has no explicit record-retention period and no separate retail rules [19]. Record-keeping may sit in other MiCA provisions or RTS. Not checked. UNVERIFIED.
- The CASP authorisation RTS asks applicants for an execution policy with a list of platforms and assessment criteria (secondary source, in French, not opened in full). UNVERIFIED.
- Application date: MiCA applied from 30 December 2024. Member states could allow a transitional period up to 1 July 2026 [25]. ESMA said unauthorised providers had to complete wind-down by 1 July 2026 [25]. Secondary sources, ESMA primary statement linked in [25].
- ESMA technical standards specifically on best-execution evidence: not found.
- The TUM brief says Article 78 lacks a consolidated tape, a duty to disclose effective spread and standardised execution-quality reporting [20].

### SEC Rule 605 as amended (US equities; firms, not users)
- Adopted 6 March 2024. It covers NMS stocks only [22].
- It widens scope to broker-dealers with 100,000 or more customer accounts and requires a separate report for single-dealer platforms [22].
- New content per SEC fact sheet [22]:
  - Order size categories based on notional dollar value and on fractional, odd-lot or round-lot status. The exact dollar thresholds I did not confirm. UNVERIFIED.
  - New order-type categories, including marketable IOC and orders with stop prices [22].
  - Realized spread at additional horizons "ranging from less than 100 microseconds to 5 minutes after the time of order receipt" [22]. One secondary source lists 50 ms, 1 s, 15 s, 1 min, 5 min. UNVERIFIED.
  - New metrics: average effective over average quoted spread; percentage-based effective and realized spreads; size-improvement benchmark and statistic; extra price-improvement statistics versus the best displayed price [22].
  - A public summary report in CSV and PDF [22].
- Compliance date: originally 18 months after effectiveness (14 December 2025). Extended to 1 August 2026 by SEC action published in the Federal Register (document 2025-19316) [23]. An April 2026 notice (2026-06569) repeats 1 August 2026 and grants limited exemptions [23]. Whether anything changed after that: not confirmed.
- Use for a retail user: none directly (reports are by firms and cover US equities). Use for a product: the metric definitions (effective spread, realized spread at horizons, E/Q) are public, reusable and recognised.

### UK FCA
- The FCA published its final cryptoasset regime policy statements on 30 June 2026 (PS26/11 for regulated activities) [24]. Law-firm summaries say best execution for intermediaries is modelled on COBS 11.2A, applies when serving retail clients, and that payment for order flow is unlikely to meet the requirements [24]. Dates differ across summaries (regime start 25 October 2027 in one). UNVERIFIED. Check the PS26/11 PDF [24].

### What a retail user can use
- None of these regimes gives a retail user a right to a per-trade execution report. MiCA gives a client a right to ask the firm to demonstrate compliance with its policy [19]. An independent audit of one's own fills is outside what the rules provide.

## 4. Retail cost studies

### TUM School of Management (verified)
- Title: "Pricing Transparency in European Crypto Trading: An Empirical Assessment of Execution Costs Across Major Retail Exchanges". Research brief, 2 pages, file dated April 2026 [20].
- Authors: Rihacek, Voigt and Heilbock; supervised by Prof. Dr. Philipp Maume (TUM) [20]. The brief's own citation line says 2025; the file and header say April 2026 [20].
- Design: Phase 1: 924 order-book observations on Bitvavo, Kraken, Coinbase over 62 days (Jul to Sep 2025). Phase 2: 50 buy-then-sell round trips of EUR 100 on six platforms over 12 trading days (Oct to Nov 2025) [20].
- Mean round-trip cost (N per platform, hidden spread in percentage points) [20]:
  - Bitvavo -0.58% (N=13, hidden 0.08)
  - BSDEX -0.88% (N=13, 0.18)
  - Bison -2.58% (N=6, 0.08)
  - Kraken -5.81% (N=6, 3.81)
  - Bitpanda -6.23% (N=6, 4.25)
  - Coinbase -7.49% (N=6, 4.51)
- Hidden spread is actual round-trip cost minus the cost predicted from the published fee schedule [20].
- Limits stated by the authors: small samples (N=6 for four platforms); descriptive of the period; EUR 100 trade size penalises fixed fees; Kraken and Coinbase also offer order-book interfaces with better execution than the retail interfaces tested [20].
- Disclosure: study done in cooperation with Bitvavo (EUR 5,000 initial funding, reimbursed, per the authors) [20]. Data and code on GitHub, repo name per the brief: dominikhei/tum-research-markets [20].
- A longer "ProjectStudy Crypto Pricing - Final Report" exists at the TUM site [28]. I did not open it.

### Frankfurt School Blockchain Center with intas.tech (verified, partly)
- News item "New study reveals significant differences in the total costs of crypto trading for retail investors", 17 March 2026 [21].
- 432 round trips (buy then immediate sell), nine MiCAR-regulated providers: Bison, Bitpanda, Bitvavo, Coinbase, flatex, justTRADE, Kraken, N26, Trade Republic. January and February 2026. Six assets (BTC, ETH, XRP, SOL, LINK, AVAX), two sizes (EUR 100 and EUR 500) [21].
- Lowest average total round-trip cost: Bitvavo 0.53%. Highest: Coinbase 6.45% [21]. The page does not list the other seven; the TUM brief quotes Frankfurt School figures of Bison 2.50% and Kraken 5.92% [20].
- The study's individual authors are not named on the news page [21]. I did not open the full report.

### Joint study
- I found no single study by TUM and Frankfurt School together. They are two independent studies. The TUM brief cites the Frankfurt School study as independent confirmation [20].

### Other measured studies
- Coinbase: the TUM and Frankfurt figures above [20][21]. Coinbase itself says the simple flow includes a spread plus any fee (support reply in an archived Reddit thread) [26].
- Robinhood: no independent measurement found. A Robinhood-commissioned study ("verified" by Radius Insights) claims lowest cost; it is commissioned and not independent (via search snippet) [27]. Blog claims of about 2% spread are unsourced measurement [27]. UNVERIFIED.
- Revolut: only blog and opinion pieces with figures from 1% to 6% [27]. UNVERIFIED. No measured study found.
- Kraken: measured in [20][21]. Bitpanda: measured in [20][21]. Trade Republic: in Frankfurt study, number not seen [21]. eToro: not found.

## 5. What traders complain about

I could not retrieve direct Reddit or Hacker News threads through the tools available. The themes below are supported by the sources named; the evidence is weaker than forum quotes would be.

- **Hidden spread on broker-style flows.** Guides, citing Reddit discussion, say the spread is inside the quoted price on Coinbase simple buy and sell [26]. Coinbase support confirms spread plus fee [26]. The measured studies back this up [20][21]. Fee figures in blogs disagree (about 0.5% versus 1%), so no number should be taken from them [26].
- **Stop-loss failures and slippage.** Self-reported complaints on a complaint site describe Binance futures stop-loss orders not executing and positions liquidated [29]. They are unverified claims and no outcome is shown [29]. Binance's own docs state a stop-loss market order becomes a market order at trigger, and a stop-limit order specifies a limit price [46]. Reports of failed stops in the October 2025 crash were found in one article and not independently confirmed [29].
- **Mark-price and liquidation disputes.** No sources found in this pass.
- **Fee-tier confusion.** Only indirect support: Coinbase has different cost structures for simple and advanced flows [26]. Binance fee-tier complaints: not found.
- **API fill data quality.** CCXT GitHub issues show recurring problems: KuCoin trades missing because two history endpoints exist, ignored `since` filters on Crypto.com, multiple fee entries per trade, and incomplete closed-order data on Binance [30]. These are library issues, not forum complaints, but they show the data-quality risk for any fills importer.
- Source types are public and no poster data is used.

Cheaper alternative:
- Sigstore Rekor v2 reached general availability (blog dated October 2025 in the feed). It uses a tile-backed log, one API endpoint, and yearly shards [42]. Public instance is free to use (UNVERIFIED for any quota). It records signed entries, so it proves "this hash existed by this time", not where the data came from.
- Ed25519 signed manifests: sign a manifest of input hashes, code version and output hashes. Standard practice, not a sourced finding here. UNVERIFIED.
- in-toto attestations: a standard format for signed statements about build or process steps. I did not fetch a source in this pass. UNVERIFIED.
- Honest limit: none of these prove the fills came from the exchange. A signed manifest proves the audit tool's own integrity and that outputs were not changed afterwards. A web proof tries to prove origin. They answer different questions.

## Synthesis

### Gap analysis (what no found tool offers a retail or prosumer crypto trader or small broker)
1. Audit of the user's own fills from a read-only key against public trade data. Vendors in section 1 serve firms, not individuals [1][2][6][8].
2. Open, documented methodology for effective spread, markouts and shortfall in crypto. Vendor methods are marketing or partial [3][4][6]. Open-source TCA is FX or equities [10][11].
3. Independent view of broker-model app costs. The two 2026 studies measured costs with manual round trips on a few platforms [20][21]. No tool lets a user measure their own app's spread. (UNVERIFIED as absolute; I found no such tool.)
4. Fee against the real fee tier, using the account's own fee fields. Not found in any tool.
5. Verifiable integrity of the report itself. No audit product found that signs outputs [section 6].
6. An MCP interface to execution audits. None found from any vendor [44].

### Feature and positioning ideas

Each idea is audit-of-the-past only, no LLM-originated numbers, read-only keys.

1. **Broker-app round-trip audit from CSV.** Import statements from broker apps and compute realized round-trip cost against the venue's public trades or candles at the same timestamps. Support: broker-model hidden spreads of 3.8 to 4.5 points over published fees [20]. Feasibility: needs minute-level reference prices; free from public archives for Binance and Bybit [31][32]; limits are matching a EUR quote to a USD reference. Pillar: Measured.
2. **Published-fee versus actual-cost gap.** Per fill, show "fee schedule predicted cost" versus "measured cost", the same definition as the TUM hidden spread [20]. Support: [20]. Feasibility: easy, uses feeUsd and tier. Pillar: Measured.
3. **Public methodology document with worked examples.** One page per metric with formulas and a hand-computed example, mirroring Rule 605 metric names (effective spread, realized spread at horizons, E/Q) [22]. Support: vendors do not publish methods [3][6]. Feasibility: documentation only. Pillar: Measured, Verifiable.
4. **Markout curves at fixed horizons for maker fills.** Mid-price change after fill at several horizons, reported as past findings. Support: maker adverse-selection research [17] and Rule 605 realized spread horizons [22]. Feasibility: needs trade data; Binance and Bybit archives give trades with aggressor flag [31][32]. Depth for mid-price is limited (see risks). Pillar: Measured.
5. **Aggressor-side agreement check.** Compare the user's fill side to the public trade labels around the same time, per venue, and report the match rate with venue conventions applied (Coinbase maker-side, Binance buyer-is-maker) [31][38]. Support: conventions differ [31][38]. Feasibility: easy. Pillar: Measured, Verifiable.
6. **Fill-data quality report.** List missing trades, duplicated fills, multi-fee entries and unit mismatches found in the user's own pull before scoring. Support: recurring API-fill problems in CCXT issues [30]. Feasibility: deterministic checks. Pillar: Measured, Verifiable.
7. **Signed evidence pack.** Ed25519-signed manifest of inputs hashes, code version, score version and outputs. Optional timestamp entry in Sigstore Rekor v2 [42]. Support: [42]; limits in section 6. Feasibility: free; Rekor public instance quota UNVERIFIED. Pillar: Verifiable.
8. **Reproduce-it-yourself bundle.** Export raw pulled fills plus the reference-data pointers and a script so a third party can recompute every number. Support: TUM published data and code for the same reason [20]. Feasibility: easy. Pillar: Verifiable, Measured.
9. **Narrative that must cite metric IDs.** The LLM explanation may only reference numbers by metric ID; the renderer substitutes the number from code. Support: no vendor was found that documents this [section 7]; fits the project rule. Feasibility: internal engineering. Pillar: Explained.
10. **Read-only MCP server exposing audit results.** Tools return past findings, with their metric IDs and hashes. Support: no TCA or exchange-audit MCP found from a vendor [44]; MCP Apps could show a chart in the client [43]. Feasibility: free. Pillar: Explained, Verifiable.
11. **Venue-versus-venue past comparison for the user's own size.** Replay the user's past fills against the archived trade tape on another venue and report what the tape would have implied, labelled as hypothetical past comparison with stated assumptions. Support: venue differences in studies [20][21]. Feasibility: moderate; must avoid advice wording. Risk: could read as "route here". Pillar: Measured.
12. **Stop-order outcome audit.** For stop fills, report trigger price versus fill price and the public trade prices in between. Support: stop-loss slippage complaints [29]. Feasibility: needs order and trigger data from the exchange API. Pillar: Measured.
13. **Honest-limits panel on every report.** State sample size, reference-data source and what could not be measured, as TUM did [20]. Support: [20]. Feasibility: easy. Pillar: Explained, Verifiable.
14. **Broker-level aggregate mode.** For a small broker, a batch audit across client accounts with export suitable as evidence of ongoing monitoring under Article 78(6) [19]. Support: [19]. Feasibility: needs consent and multi-account handling. Compliance claims must be avoided (see risks). Pillar: Measured, Verifiable.

### Risks and honest limits

Legal:
- I found no source that decides whether a past-execution audit is "investment advice". It is a question for a lawyer. Under MiCA, advice is a defined regulated service; the rules I read do not address third-party audit tools [19]. UNVERIFIED. Keep copy past-tense and avoid recommendations.
- Do not claim the report satisfies MiCA Article 78 or Rule 605. Those duties sit with firms [19][22]. A claim of "compliance evidence" would be a legal risk.
- Reading someone's fills is personal financial data. GDPR applies to EU users (general knowledge; not sourced here). UNVERIFIED.
- Scraping or using exchange APIs is subject to each exchange's terms. Not checked.

Data:
- No free historical spot order book on Binance, Bybit, Kraken or Coinbase was found. Quoted spread at the fill time then has to come from trades or candles, which gives proxies, not true quotes. OKX lists order-book history from March 2023 [34]; spot coverage unknown.
- Binance timestamps changed to microseconds for spot data from 2025-01-01 [31]. A parser must handle both units.
- Bybit CSV columns changed (an `rpi` column appears in recent files) [32].
- Kraken has no bulk archive and Coinbase depth is not stated [36][38].
- Broker apps quote in EUR and may not trade on the reference venue, so a hidden-spread measure is a comparison to a reference, not to the app's own book [20].
- Studies show N=6 per platform for most brokers [20]; do not generalise.

Competitive:
- Talos, CoinRoutes and Kaiko already sell TCA or benchmarks to the regulated firm segment [3][6][8]. They could add a retail or prosumer product. I found no sign of this.
- Exchanges could add their own cost tools (one search result mentioned an exchange launching a slippage tool, unnamed, UNVERIFIED).
- zkTLS projects are moving fast, but today they are pre-production or secondary-sourced [40][41]. Claiming "proof of origin" before it works would damage trust.
