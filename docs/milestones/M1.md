## What a user gets
- **Every Binance fill is measured against real public prints.** The measurements are the effective spread, the realized spread and the price impact at 1, 15, 60 and 300 s, and effective over quoted. The mid comes from aggressor-side trades strictly before the fill. When no print is fresh, the fill reads "not measured"; nothing is filled in.
- **Every order gets an implementation shortfall** (Perold 1988): execution, split into spread and impact/timing, plus the unfilled cost and the fees. Orders that never filled are measured too.
- **Fees are compared with the account's own commission rates.** The rates come from Binance's commission endpoint, and the formula reproduces the exchange's worked example exactly.
- **A measured score (v2) sits beside the rule-based score (v1).** It comes with a 95% bootstrap range. No letter grade is shown below 20 measured fills; the range and the reason are shown instead.
- **Each v2 score has an integrity hash.** "Verify integrity" recomputes it from the stored inputs and the committed version file, and it also recomputes the score itself.
- **A `/methodology` page is generated from the code.** A test fails if the page and the code drift apart.

## Measured results (sources and commands in `docs/research/LOG.md`)
- **Real fill check.** A pretend buy at BTCUSDT print 2830846703 (2024-01-15) measured an effective half-spread of 0.005/42864.245 × 1e4 = 0.0011665 bps (half of one tick), with E/Q 1.0. BNBUSDT the same day measured 1.56814 bps, also half of its 0.1 tick.
- **Calibration v2.0.0.** 20 checksum-verified daily files: BTC, ETH, BNB and SOL against USDT, every 7th day from 2025-10-01 to 2025-10-29. For fills under 1k USD, the median public taker print paid 0.2 bps (BTC) to 0.45 bps (SOL) effective half-spread. All four symbols cost most at 14:00 UTC.
- **Hand-checked cases reproduced end to end.** The order shortfall totals 12.0 bps (execution 2.0, unfilled cost 2.5, fees 7.5). The Commission FAQ rate is 0.06000245. The scoring case is composite 57.825 (grade D).

## What changed in the plan, and why
- **Outbox.** The trade rows are the outbox; there is no job collection inside a transaction (ADR-0011). It is simpler, a crash loses nothing, and it is idempotent.
- **Service token.** It is signed on the existing `JWT_ACCESS_SECRET` with type `measure`, so no new credential was needed.
- **No "assumed" fee rate is ever stored.** The plan had one; it would have been an invented number.
- **No `liquidity` field.** The trade's existing `isMaker` already records it.
- **Unfilled orders are measured,** which covers "the cost of orders that didn't fill".
- **Bybit and OKX orders are not built.** OKX order-history facts could not be confirmed from the official page, and Bybit keeps only filled orders older than 7 days. Their fills carry an `unmeasuredReason` in this version.
- **Integrity covers fee snapshots and order measurements** as well as fills, and verification recomputes the result.
- **A 4R-1a plan correction.** In fixture W the +1 s horizon has a fresh mid (100.05); only +60 s and +300 s are `None`.

## Review (independent `reviewer` subagent on the whole M1 diff)
No high findings. All medium and low findings were fixed, each with a test:
1. **(Medium) Verification did not check the stored result.** It now recomputes the score from the stored inputs and compares it (`test_verify_detects_an_edited_result`).
2. **(Medium) A busy-hour merged window could fail forever.** It now falls back to one window per fill, and too-recent fills no longer take batch slots.
3. **(Medium) The latest fee snapshot was applied to old fills.** A snapshot now applies only within 30 days of a fill, and the methodology page says so.
4. **(Low) The methodology text claimed one mapping for all components.** Each component's mapping is now generated from `scoring_v2/components.py`.
5. **(Low) Open orders kept a stale end.** They are measured again once final.
6. **(Low) The unfilled cost could be overstated** when the stored fills do not add up to the executed quantity. Such an order now reads "not measured".
7. **(Low) "Slippage" could be misread.** The methodology now says it is the mid move from order arrival to the fills, mostly market movement for fast fills.

## M1.1 corrections (2026-11-12, after the architect review)

**What changed for a user.** The spread part of the measured score now compares a trader's order with other people's orders, measured the same way. Before, the public baseline counted each price level of one large order as a separate small trade, so it looked about 400 times more expensive than the market really was for BTC. The baseline now comes from 90 days of data instead of 5. The link between the two services has its own secret, and CI scans the whole git history for secrets.

**1. Spread baseline bias: found, explained and fixed** (ADR-0012, `docs/research/LOG.md` §K, `ml-service/scripts/spread_bias_report.py`).
- On the 20 v2.0.0 files, prints under 1k USD: BTC median 0.204 bps overall; 0.00045 bps (half a tick) for the first print of a same-millisecond same-side run; 0.382 bps for the later prints, which were 64% of the prints. Grouped into taker events, the BTC median is half a tick at every quote age.
- Quote age is a second-order effect. Moving the cap from 5 s to 1 s changes event medians by less than 0.012 bps in 13 of 16 cells, and tighter caps keep only 5-37% of events. **Decision: keep 5 s** on both the fill and the baseline, and record both proxy ages on every fill.
- The baseline is now built from taker events (`scoring_v2/calibrate.py` `taker_events`). User fills are scored in the same unit: the fills of one order in one millisecond, with one side and one mid, form one event (`score.py`, when the version says `"spreadUnit": "taker_event"`). v2.0.0 still scores each fill separately, so its results stay reproducible.
- A test confirms user fills are unaffected. All fills of one order share the quote from before their millisecond, and their quantity-weighted value equals the public event value exactly.

**2. Calibration v2.1.0 over 90 days** (every day, 2026-08-12 to 2026-11-09; BTC, ETH, BNB and SOL against USDT).
- 360 checksum-verified files, streamed into day summaries. Each raw file was deleted after its summary was saved.
- Every anchor stores its sample count. Every hour of every profile has 90 days of samples; the new minimum is 30.
- lt1k medians: BTC 0.200 → 0.00063 bps, ETH 0.343 → 0.032, BNB 0.267 → 0.071, SOL 0.451 → 0.498. All four symbols still cost most at 14:00 UTC.
- `SCORING_V2_VERSION` = v2.1.0, pinned at `63e20f1d…`. The methodology page is regenerated, and the measurement version is unchanged (1.0.0).

**3. Service-token secret.** `ML_SERVICE_TOKEN_SECRET` replaces `JWT_ACCESS_SECRET` for the backend → ml-service token, with no fallback.
- The secret is unusable when it is missing, shorter than 32 characters, or equal to `JWT_ACCESS_SECRET`.
- Then the ml-service answers 503 on `/internal/*`, after checking the header. The backend sends nothing, and logs one warning that names the key and the reason, never the value.
- Waiting for Deepanshu: set it on both Render services (PROGRESS item 6).

**4. Secret scan in CI.** New job `secrets`: gitleaks 8.30.1, pinned and checksum-verified, scans the full history.
- `.gitleaks.toml` adds a rule for passwords inside MongoDB URIs, which the default rules miss. That rule found the six rotated Atlas URIs of commit `1bd5be8a`.
- `.gitleaksignore` lists 14 known findings, each with its reason.
- A negative control (a fake Atlas URI and a fake GitHub token) is caught; placeholders pass.
