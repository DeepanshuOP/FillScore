# M4 — Explain and act

Plan: `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-17-m4-explain-and-act.md`. Everything in M4 is deterministic, read-only on stored measurements, and in past tense. No model writes a number or a sentence here (invariants I1, I2, I8).

## What a user gets

- **An exception queue.** The orders worth a second look, at most 20, dearest in USD first, each with one line built from its stored numbers. Three rules, each with its threshold stated: a market order whose execution cost was above the upper end of its expected interval; an order that never filled and cost more than the account's own 90th percentile; a filled order whose total cost was above that percentile. The percentile rules need 20 measured orders; with fewer the queue says "insufficient data" and does not guess.
- **Review states.** Each queued order can be marked reviewed, dismissed or reopened, per account. A demo account sees the queue and has no controls (the server also refuses any change on a demo account).
- **An order replay.** Every figure of one order (execution, spread, impact and timing, unfilled part, fees, total, each in bps and USD) names the stored field it came from. The market data behind it is linked to its public file (data.binance.vision daily file, or the REST window) with its SHA-256, so a third party can fetch and check it. A figure that was not computed reads "not computed", never zero.
- **Rankings that say "too few to tell".** Execution cost by symbol or by hour of the day, with a bootstrap interval; a group with fewer than 20 orders is not ranked, and groups whose intervals overlap share a rank and are listed as not distinguishable.
- **A matched venue comparison.** The account's own past fills, matched by symbol and size bucket, never pooled; with fills on one venue only it says there is nothing to compare. A replay of past fills on another venue's tape is not built: it would read as routing advice.
- **An integrity badge.** The v2 panel shows "Verified" only after the verify route answered `match: true` in that session; a demo grade shows "Offline build, hash recorded".

## Measured results and checks

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Queue boundary cases (hand-computed) | p90 of 1..20 bps is 18.1 and the 19 and 20 bps orders are listed ($19.00 and $20.00); an order equal to the percentile is not; 19 orders give "insufficient data"; 210 orders give 21 matches and 20 listed |
| Replay USD | 22 bps x 10,000 USD / 10,000 = $22.00; the terms sum to the total |
| Ranking | constant groups: ranks 1, 1, 3; 19 orders is too few and 20 is enough |
| Routes | account isolation (another account's order never shows or changes), demo refused with 403 on change, 401 without a token |

## What changed in the plan, and why

- Not built: the fee-schedule gap per fill (A12). It needs the published fee table per fill, which the measurement does not store. Open.
- Not built: the stop-order outcome audit (A13). The order history the connector stores has no stop price. Dropped with this note.
- The rankings use execution cost over all measured orders (limit and market). A ranking over market orders only would need more orders per group than the demos have.
- The panels are minimal and use today's styles. M8 redesigns them with Deepanshu.

## Review

Independent `reviewer` subagent on the M4 diff. It found no isolation, authorization or invariant defect (every query is scoped by the token's account or the demo allowlist; the same order id on two accounts cannot cross; a demo account cannot change a review; the percentile, rank and tie rules and the replay arithmetic match the hand values). One high, one medium and several low findings, all fixed with tests (red first where it applied):

| # | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 1 high | The bootstrap in `medianInterval` sorted the whole group 2,000 times; a 20,000-order group took 15 s and froze the server (measured: 15.4 s in the failing test). | The median is still of all values; the bootstrap runs on a deterministic stride sample of at most 400 values (the interval is a little wider, never narrower). A 20,000-order group answers in under a second. |
| 2 medium | The replay was keyed by order id only, so the same id on two symbols opened both; old responses and old account data could show. | Keyed by symbol and order id; a late answer is dropped; the panels remount when the account or the score changes. |
| 3 low | The heading "Orders worth a second look" read like a call to action. | "Orders that met a rule · measured"; the fixed headings are a tested constant. |
| 4 low | The queue and review state ignore the exchange. | Latent until a second venue is measured (M6). Noted there. |
| 5 low | The venue comparison pooled makers and takers, and counted a venue with no measured cost. | Only venues with a measured cost count; the maker and taker mix is still pooled and the comparison text says it matches by symbol and size only. |
| 6 low | "Verified" could stay on a later hash. | The score panel remounts on a new integrity hash. |
| 7 low | The queue loaded every field of every measurement. | Projection of four fields. |
| 8 low | A bad stored window time threw in the replay. | Guarded; test. |
| 9 low | Only the first rule's sentence was shown. | All sentences are shown. |

Added tests for the gaps it listed: the 409 for an order id on two symbols, malformed and repeated `symbol`, a two-venue comparison with account isolation, a venue with no measured cost, and the heading copy.
