## Findings

- **zkTLS.** TLSNotary is v0.1.0-beta.1 and its README says it should not be used in production; Reclaim needs a trusted attestor, has no
  full trade-history provider, and its pricing could not be confirmed. A transcript proof shows "this response came from that server", never
  completeness. Measured: `api.binance.com` negotiates TLS 1.3 and accepts TLS 1.2. The signed evidence packs (M5) already prove that
  FillScore's measurements were not changed; they do not prove where the trades came from, and the pack says so.
- **The package cannot drift.** Its Python files are produced from `ml-service/measure/*` by a script; a test fails if they differ, and another
  runs package and service side by side on a grid of 250 input combinations with identical results required.
- **MCP App.** The page makes no request, declares no network origin, renders text only, ignores messages from any window but its host, and
  shows hostile text as text (tested). It reuses the dashboard's wording for cost vs expected.
- **DP.** The mechanism, its sensitivity argument and its limits (floating-point noise needs a snapping or discrete mechanism before real use)
  are in ADR-0017.

## What changed in the plan, and why

- **The EVM DEX audit is not built.** Three things stand in the way, none of them code: (1) the event signatures and field layouts of the
  swap events must be taken from the exchange's official documentation and checked, not remembered (rule I7); (2) it needs a source of
  blocks and logs: a keyless public RPC is rate-limited and unreliable, and a provider with a free tier needs a sign-up, which is the owner's
  decision; (3) there is no real wallet to validate a detector against, and a pattern detector that is not validated must not be shown
  as a finding. Wording when it is built: "pattern consistent with", never an accusation.
