**Market research** (`docs/research/MARKET_2026-11.md`, 46 sources). The findings that matter most:
1. No vendor found serves a retail or prosumer trader with an audit of their own fills. Bloomberg, Talos, CoinRoutes and Kaiko sell to firms, and Talos and CoinRoutes analyse orders sent through their own routers. Open-source TCA libraries cover FX and equities only.
2. Two separate 2026 studies measured retail round-trip costs by hand: TUM (−0.58% to −7.49%, at most 13 round trips per platform) and Frankfurt School (0.53% to 6.45%, 432 round trips). I re-checked both against the primary pages.
3. The TUM "hidden spread", which is the measured cost minus the cost the fee schedule predicts, reached 4.51 percentage points. FillScore can compute the same number for each fill.
4. MiCA Article 78 and SEC Rule 605 put duties on firms. FillScore can reuse the Rule 605 metric names, but must never claim that its output makes anyone compliant.
5. Proof of where trades came from (zkTLS) is not ready: TLSNotary is pre-1.0, and no project was shown to work with signed exchange API requests. A signed pack with a public timestamp is feasible today, and proves integrity and date, not origin.
6. Bybit publishes spot trade archives with an aggressor side column, so a Bybit demo from real prints is possible in Milestone 2. The column's meaning needs a test first. Kraken has no bulk archive.

**Limits of the research.** It was one pass on one day by a research subagent. Not found or not confirmed: any vendor price, Botminds, direct forum threads about trader complaints, the exact Rule 605 size buckets, a MiCA record-retention period, and whether an audit of past trades could count as investment advice anywhere. That last question needs a lawyer.
